## Abstract

In artificial intelligence (AI), the quality of training data is a key determinant of model performance. However, acquiring well-labeled and context-relevant datasets remains a challenge, particularly in the field of acoustic recognition, where labeled data is often scarce and environmental conditions vary significantly. While data augmentation is a common remedy, not all augmentation methods are equally effective or suitable for specific applications.This study introduces a methodology for constructing robust and context-relevant datasets that support high model performance, even in data-limited scenarios and across different signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) conditions. The proposed approach involves filtering and statistically enriching environmental noise specifically for the task of bird sound recognition. We present a curated dataset comprising ten European bird species, annotated both temporally and spectrally, along with the corresponding codebase and noise resources used in the augmentation process. The dataset contains 20 hours of audio, including 7 hours and 45 minutes of active bird vocalizations.Experimental results show that although models trained on the augmented data exhibit a slight performance drop on clean audio, they significantly outperform baseline models under real-world noisy conditions. This demonstrates the importance of environmentally grounded data augmentation in building resilient acoustic recognition systems.
