## Abstract

Birds primarily communicate using acoustic signals (sounds). Every bird has different sound patterns due to genetic and environmental factors such as noise. One bird species with various patterns is the Sooty-headed Bulbul (Pycnonotus aurigaster). This study aims to identify the number of vocalization patterns of the Sooty-headed Bulbul inhabiting a human-dominated habitat in Dramaga Campus, Bogor. The study was conducted from April to June 2023 at two locations with different noise levels. The passive acoustic monitoring (PAM) method was applied in this study. One unit of SwiftOne was placed for three days in each habitat at 1,5m height, with a microphone gain of 28 dB and sample rate of 32kHz. The sound analysis was conducted using RavenPro application by identifying sound parameters (frequency and duration) and spectrogram images. The results showed that there were 19 types of sound patterns consisting of 7 short phrase and 12 long phrases with an average frequency range of 2-7 kHz and a duration of 0.2-11 seconds. The most frequent patterns are the “cuk-cuk” and “crek-crek-kur”. Higher noise interference resulted in lower number of phrase variations found and frequency of the Sooty-headed Bulbul vocalization. Appropriate management actions need to be taken to reduce the impact of noise on the environment and wildlife.
