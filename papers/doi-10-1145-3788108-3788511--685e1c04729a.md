## Abstract

Fine-grained classification aims to identify the types of objects in subcategories, but because the differences between classes are very small, it is a very challenging task. Previous research works on this issue mostly focused on how to locate the most discriminative image regions, and rely on them to improve the ability of the network to capture subtle differences. However, this classification method ignores the hierarchy of object categories. The so-called hierarchical structure means that an object belongs to a specific category at different levels. For example, birds can be classified according to four levels of order, family, genus, and species. This hierarchy contains rich correlations between different levels and categories. For the task of fine-grained visual classification, which needs to capture the subtle differences between classes, the information contained in the hierarchy helps to regularize the semantic space and make prediction less ambiguous. This paper presents a hierarchical semantic embedding framework and transplants it to the neural network to predict the categories of different levels of objects and uses the high-level prediction score vector to guide the classification of lower levels so that the neural network can learn fine-grained feature differences in the training process. In addition, Visual Transformer (ViT) shows strong performance in computer vision. The stronger the discrimination, the stronger the importance of the region, which is very conducive to fine-grained visual classification. Generally speaking, this paper has constructed a hierarchical fine-grained visual classification model based on ViT. The model is compared with Transformer in CUB-200-2011 and VegFru to show its superior performance.
