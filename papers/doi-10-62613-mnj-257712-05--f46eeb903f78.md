## Abstract

An avian study was conducted at Universiti Malaya campus from October 2023 to March 2024 to examine the bird diversity across four distinct habitats: forest (three sites), aquatic (five sites), shrubby (four sites), and open field areas (four sites). The objectives were to document and compare the abundance, species diversity, richness, and evenness of birds between these four habitat types. Using point transect sampling method, birds were observed visually using binoculars and a digital camera, and supported by audio recordings. Observations were conducted in the morning (0700 hours – 1000 hours) and afternoon (1600 hours – 1900 hours), with three sampling occasions per site. Identification was based on visual characteristics, such as body colour and morphology, aided by field guides. A total of 62 species from 31 families were recorded. The highest bird species diversity and evenness were recorded in the forested habitat (H’ = 1.32342; Ds = 0.92612; J’ = 0.8571), followed by shrubby (H’ = 1.20815; Ds = 0.91577; J’ = 0.85383), aquatic habitat (H’ = 1.20537; Ds = 0.89900; J’ = 0.84212) and open field area (H’ = 1.10118; Ds = 0.87012; J’ = 0.7693). Forested habitat has the highest species richness with 35 species of the 155 individuals, likely due to the dense foliage providing plentiful perching sites and foraging opportunities for various bird species. Open field area showed the highest bird abundance with 389 individuals of 27 species, suggesting that the simplified habitat structure favours species that are well-adapted to open landscapes and are present in large numbers. Aquatic habitat recorded 156 individuals of 27 species, while shrubby habitat has 172 individuals of 26 species. This study highlights the vital role of urban green spaces in the conservation of bird diversity and offers insights for promoting species diversity through the preservation of heterogeneous habitats within the urban landscape.
