## Abstract

Declining bird numbers during the last halfcentury have highlighted the urgent need for precise and noninvasive monitoring and conservation techniques. To overcome this difficulty, this study presents an automated approach for classifying bird species that makes use of machine learning and audio processing methods. The method efficiently separates species based on distinct audio patterns while filtering background noise by analyzing bird cries using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and extracting important information using Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs). The technique improves its appropriateness for field applications by providing a less intrusive substitute for conventional image-based methods. Three bird species American Redstart, Common Tern, and Red-winged Blackbird are correctly classified using the system’s capacity to analyze unique vocalizations. To improve robustness and dependability, strategies including model regularization and data augmentation were used. Although issues like background noise, bird call variability, and audio representation continue to be major barriers, the strategy highlights how sophisticated audio processing can help boost biodiversity research and conservation efforts around the world.
