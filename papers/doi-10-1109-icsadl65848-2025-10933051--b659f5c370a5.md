## Abstract

Acoustic monitoring of rare and elusive bird species presents significant challenges due to the complexity of environmental noise and the variability in bird vocalizations. This study proposes a novel deep learning framework, BioAudio VisionNet (BAVNet), specifically designed for the acoustic identification and monitoring of the Himalayan Monal and other rare bird species. The performance of the proposed algorithm was evaluated against three established algorithms: Support Vector Machines with Acoustic Features (SVM-AF), Audio Spectrogram Transformer (ASF), and Deep Belief Networks (DBN). Simulation experiments were conducted using metrics such as accuracy versus the number of epochs, processing time versus accuracy, and spectrogram classification accuracy versus spectrogram resolution. The results demonstrated that BAVNet achieved a notable improvement in classification accuracy, computational efficiency, and adaptability to varying spectrogram resolutions when compared to existing methods. These findings highlight the potential of BAVNet for real-time biodiversity monitoring in challenging environments. Future work can explore the integration of additional contextual data, such as environmental factors, to further enhance system performance.
