## Abstract

ABSTRACT Capsule Urban pressures, including artificial lights, traffic noise and urbanization, influenced the song timing and duration of Eurasian Blackbirds and European Robins during the dawn chorus. Aims To disentangle and prioritize the effects of artificial lights, traffic noise, urbanization and conspecific abundance on the song timing and duration of Eurasian Blackbirds and European Robins along an urbanization gradient. Methods We used passive acoustic monitoring and artificial intelligence (AI) to automatically detect the song timing of Eurasian Blackbirds and European Robins from midnight to noon. We analysed the effects of human pressures (artificial lights, traffic noise, urbanization) and conspecific abundance on the median time and duration of the song. Results Eurasian Blackbirds sang earlier and for shorter durations as traffic noise increased. In contrast, European Robins sang earlier with higher levels of artificial light and for shorter periods in highly urbanized areas. Conclusion Birds tend to sing earlier and for shorter periods in human-dominated landscapes. Nevertheless, empirical studies are still needed to rank the relative influence of each pressure on different species.
