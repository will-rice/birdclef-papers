## Abstract

The Snoring Rail (Aramidopsis plateni) is a Sulawesi endemic bird that is seriously threatened. Only 20 individuals were recorded in previous surveys conducted across buffer villages surrounding Gandang Dewata National Park. A population density of 1–3 individuals per hectare, recorded in 2023, underscores its critical conservation status. This study aims to investigate its activity patterns, vocal behavior, and to monitor the species’ population, distribution and the environmental factors influencing its presence. Data were collected using AudioMoth recorders at six points across four buffer villages: Taupe, Tondok Bakaru, Rambusaratu, and Mambuliling. Habitat surveys were conducted with the aid of binoculars and field guides. Bird vocalizations were analyzed using BirdNET and Raven Pro, while population and distribution data were processed using Generalized Linear Models in R and QGIS. Acoustic detections confirmed the species’ presence at all sites, with most calls recorded in the morning and at midday. Detection confidence values ranged from 0.5 to 0.9665, indicating high classification accuracy. The number of observed individuals ranged from 1 to 5 per site, with Taupe and Tondok Bakaru identified as current hotspots. The species’ restricted and fragmented distribution highlights the urgent need for targeted conservation efforts to prevent its extinction in the wild.
