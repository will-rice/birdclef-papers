## Abstract

Bird sound detection provides valuable insights into regional biodiversity, supporting the development of effective conservation strategies through monitoring of bird population densities, migration patterns, and overall biodiversity. Our methodology converts bird audio recordings into Mel spectrograms, which are then utilized as input for a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model. This deep learning approach enables the CNN to learn discriminative features from the spectrograms, leading to accurate classification of bird species calls. Our novel system, employing CNNs, achieves an accuracy rate on the training dataset and an 84% accuracy rate on the validation dataset. Implemented as a web-based interface, this model predicts the bird species from audio recordings, providing a scalable and powerful tool for ecological research and conservation efforts. This system enhances our understanding of avian biodiversity and supports targeted conservation initiatives. Furthermore, its adaptability allows for application in various ecological contexts, facilitating large-scale environmental monitoring. The integration of this technology can significantly advance our knowledge of avian populations and contribute to more effective biodiversity conservation strategies.
