## Abstract

Nowadays, the traditional acoustic inspection relies on manual analysis, which suffers from low efficiency, limited coverage, and rarely undergoes systematic optimization from the perspective of acoustic fingerprint recognition. The study developed a deep learning-based soundprint recognition system for bird song characteristics. The system employs Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCC) to extract key acoustic features, utilizing spectral subtraction for noise reduction and MFCC normalization as preprocessing steps, developing convolutional neural network (CNN) models to process large-scale bird sound data. The test results indicate that under certain noise conditions, the recognition accuracy exceeds 90%, with approximately two hundred thousand parameters and a latency below 3 seconds, during the model evaluation phase, analysis of the test dataset revealed that the system achieved a recall rate of 85% and an F1 score of 0.82. The study provides an efficient and intelligent technical pathway for automated bird monitoring and biodiversity conservation, demonstrating potential for field deployment of portable devices.
