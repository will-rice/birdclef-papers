## Abstract

Monitoring bird and bat populations is essential for understanding ecosystem health, particularly in areas affected by human activities such as land and offshore wind farm development. Passive acoustic monitoring provides a scalable, noninvasive method for detecting and tracking vocalizations from a variety of bird and bat species. Compared to single- microphone systems, microphone arrays offer both signal enhancement and directional sensing in airborne acoustic systems, significantly improving the detectability and localization of sound sources. These capabilities also support integration with complementary systems-such as cameras or thermal sensors for multi-modal ecological monitoring. A 16-element prototype microphone array has been developed and assembled in-house at Northeastern University. Each microphone has a linear frequency response between 20 Hz and 75 kHz. The system has 16 individual Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) channels, each with a sampling rate of 192 kHz and a 24-bit resolution, allowing the capture of sounds up to 96 kHz. This paper shows the results of a test that was conducted at an open field bordered by forest located on the North Shore of Massachusetts. It showcases the advantages of using an array instead of a single microphone such as the ability to track the bearing of different vocalizations over time and the enhancement of the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)
